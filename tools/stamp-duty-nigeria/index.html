Stamp duty in Nigeria

Nearly every rate you will find online for this is now wrong. The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 replaced the old stamp duties schedule, so figures written before 2026 describe a law that no longer applies. The rates below are read from the Act. The most useful thing in it is an exemption: a lease whose annual value is under ₦10,000,000 is not chargeable with duty at all, which takes most Nigerian tenancies out of this entirely.

Last checked 23 November 2026. Source: Nigeria Tax Act, 2025 (2025 No. 7) — sections 126, 135 and 136 and the duty table. General information, not legal or tax advice, and the Act leaves several questions to the relevant tax authority.

The exemption most people miss

Section 135 says a lease agreement is charged as if it were an actual lease for the term and consideration in it — and then takes most of them out again:

“provided that lease agreements for which the annual value is less than ₦10,000,000 or 10 times the annual minimum wage, whichever is higher, shall not be chargeable with any duty under Chapter Five of this Act.”

So an ordinary residential tenancy — even a fairly expensive one — is usually not chargeable. Two things to hold onto. The threshold is the higher of the two limbs, so if ten times the annual minimum wage ever exceeds ₦10,000,000, that becomes the figure; we have not sourced the current minimum wage from a primary source, so this page applies the ₦10,000,000 limb and says so rather than quietly assuming. And it is the annual value, not the total over the term.

There is a similar exemption on a sale: a conveyance or transfer on sale is exempt where the property has a value of ₦10,000,000 or less. The Act also exempts transfers between associated companies holding at least 90% of each other, directly or through a third party, subject to a proviso.

You have 30 days

Section 126(1): an instrument executed in Nigeria that is chargeable with duty “shall be stamped not later than 30 days after its execution by the person required to pay the appropriate duty.” Under the old law it was forty. If you are working to a closing checklist written before 2026, that is ten days you no longer have.

One rate we are not printing

Being confirmed — leases above seven years

The Act’s duty table gives the lease rate as “Up to 7 years = 0.78%. Above 7” — and the cell stops there in the copy of the Act we read. The continuation is absent from the document’s text, so we do not know from the Act what the rate above seven years is. Commentary disagrees: some gives a single higher band, some gives one band to 21 years and another above it. We are not choosing between them on your behalf. If you hold a clean copy of that row, send it to hello@deeluh.com and we will publish it with the date we read it.

What each of these instruments is

Conveyance or transfer on sale

The instrument that moves ownership of land or property on a sale — in practice the deed of assignment. Charged at 1.5% and payable by the transferee, which is to say the buyer. This is the big one in most property files.

A lease or tenancy

Charged on the lease or sublease, or on the assignment of a lease, with the duty turning on the term. The lessee pays. Read the exemption above before you calculate anything.

A mortgage, and a conveyance by way of security

Both at 0.375%, and in both cases it is the party taking the security who pays. A conveyance that looks absolute but is really security for money is treated as the security it is, not as a sale — which is why labelling a document carelessly can change the duty by a factor of four.

Capital duty on nominal shares

0.75% of the share capital, payable by the company, and separate from what the Corporate Affairs Commission charges to register the company in the first place. Our CAC fees page covers that half.

Where this comes from

The Nigeria Tax Act, 2025 (2025 No. 7): section 126 for the 30-day obligation, section 135 for leases and their exemption, section 136 for share capital, and the Act’s duty table for the rates and for who is liable to pay each one. An extract of the provisions this page relies on is committed alongside it, and a check compares the figures published here against that extract on every change.

Pages still quoting a flat 6% on tenancies, or rates attributed to the Stamp Duties Act, describe the position before January 2026. If the revenue authority publishes a schedule that differs from the Act as we have read it, tell us and we will correct this and change the date at the top.
