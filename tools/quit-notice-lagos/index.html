Quit notice in Lagos

How much notice depends on the kind of tenancy, not on how long somebody has lived there — and before any of that, on where the property is. The Tenancy Law 2011 does not apply everywhere in Lagos, which is the first thing this asks and the thing most notices get wrong.

Last checked 23 November 2026. Periods from section 13 of the Tenancy Law of Lagos State 2011. General information, not legal advice. A notice served wrongly can cost a landlord months, so if anything here is close to the line, take advice before serving.

Work out the notice

Asked first because it decides which law applies at all.

Generally decided by when rent is paid or demanded, not by what the agreement calls it.

The periods, and where they come from

Section 13(1) applies “where there is no stipulation as to the notice to be given by either party to determine the tenancy” — so it is a default, not a floor. An agreed period displaces it.

Section 13(1), Tenancy Law of Lagos State 2011
Kind of tenancyNotice
A tenant at willOne week
A monthly tenantOne month
A quarterly tenantThree months
A half-yearly tenantThree months
A yearly tenantSix months

A half-yearly tenant gets the same three months as a quarterly one. That looks like a misprint and is not: it is what the subsection says, and it is the single most commonly mis-stated period in this area.

Six months is not every tenant’s right

The belief that every tenant in Lagos is entitled to six months’ notice is the most expensive misunderstanding here, and it runs both ways. A monthly tenant is entitled to one month, not six. A landlord who serves six months on a monthly tenant has not broken the law, but has given away five months. A landlord who serves one month on a yearly tenant has served a bad notice and will usually have to start again.

Where the Tenancy Law does not apply

The Tenancy Law 2011 applies across Lagos State except Apapa, Ikeja GRA, Ikoyi and Victoria Island, which remain under the Recovery of Premises Law. Those are exactly the areas where high-value commercial and residential tenancies sit, so this is not an obscure carve-out — it is the carve-out most likely to bite a lawyer drafting from a template. This tool refuses to give a period for them rather than giving the wrong one.

We have this from practitioners’ published commentary rather than from the face of the statute, so treat the boundary as a thing to confirm for the specific property, not a line to rely on blind.

After the notice to quit

The notice to quit ends the tenancy. It does not get anybody out. If the tenant stays, the landlord must then serve a seven-day notice of intention to apply to recover possession before going to court — and under section 13(5) that seven-day notice is required even where a fixed term has already expired by effluxion of time and no notice to quit was needed at all.

Two timing points from section 13(4): notices for quarterly, half-yearly and yearly tenants need not end on the anniversary of the tenancy, and may take effect on or after the date the tenancy expires. This is a relief if you have ever been told a notice must land on an exact date.

One question we have put to a lawyer rather than answered

Being confirmed — the weekly tenant

The text of section 13(1) we read lists a tenant at will, a monthly, quarterly, half-yearly and yearly tenant. It does not list a weekly tenant separately. Secondary sources state one week for a weekly tenancy, which is plausible — a weekly tenant may well be a tenant at will for this purpose — but that is an inference, and inferring a statutory notice period is how a bad notice gets served. If you pick “a weekly tenant” above, the tool says this instead of printing a figure.

Where this comes from

  • Section 13, Tenancy Law of Lagos State 2011 — the subsection text, for the periods and for subsections (4) and (5)
  • Nigerian practitioners’ published commentary for the excluded areas, and for the note that a 2025 bill to amend the Lagos tenancy rules has not become law
