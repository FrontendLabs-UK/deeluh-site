CAC registration fees in 2026

Most of the figures online for this are out of date, and they disagree with each other. The numbers below are the Commission’s own, read from the gazetted New Schedule of Fees — so a company with share capital is ₦10,000 for every ₦1,000,000 or part thereof, and the words “or part thereof” are where people get the number wrong.

Last checked 23 November 2026. Source: Corporate Affairs Commission, New Schedule of Fees, S.I. No. 6, Federal Republic of Nigeria Official Gazette No. 92, Vol. 112, Lagos, 29 May 2025, dated 31 October 2024. This is general information, not legal or tax advice.

Work out the Commission’s fees

Charged per whole or part million, so ₦1,000,001 costs the same as ₦2,000,000.

Registration already includes a certified true copy of the registration documents. This is for additional ones, at ₦5,000 each.

What “or part thereof” actually costs you

The schedule charges a company with share capital per million, rounding up. So the difference between ₦1,000,000 and ₦1,000,001 of share capital is not one naira of fee — it is ten thousand, because the second figure is two part-millions rather than one. If your share capital is a round number for no particular reason, this is the one place where choosing it carelessly costs real money.

It applies again later: registering an increase in issued share capital, up to ₦500 million, is charged at the same rate on the same rounding.

What the Commission’s fee does not include

The total above is what the Commission charges. Three things sit outside it, and people are caught by all three:

  • Stamp duty. Duty on share capital is a tax matter, collected separately from registration, and the rates changed with the 2025 tax legislation. We are not printing a 2026 figure for it until we can source it from the revenue authority.
  • Whoever files it for you. A lawyer’s or accredited agent’s fee is their own and is not in any gazette.
  • Later obligations. Annual returns are a recurring fee, and the schedule carries penalties for filing them late — including a daily default penalty. Registering is the cheap part.

Which kind of entity is which

A business name

A registered trading name, not a separate legal person. It is the cheapest thing on the list, and the thing most people actually mean when they say they want to “register a business”. It gives you no limited liability.

A company with share capital, small or otherwise

A separate legal person, and the fee turns on the share capital rather than on a flat rate. Whether a company counts as “small” is a statutory test, not a choice you make on the form — and the registration rate in the schedule is the same for a small company and a private company other than small, so getting the label wrong does not change this total. It changes other fees, including annual returns.

A company limited by guarantee

No share capital, so a flat fee, and its registration fee already includes the certified true copy of the first incorporation documents. Used for not-for-profit bodies that want a company form.

Incorporated trustees

The form most NGOs, churches, mosques and associations use. It is a registration of trustees rather than of a company, and both its name reservation and its registration cost more than a company limited by guarantee’s name reservation — worth knowing before you assume the charitable route is the cheap one.

Where this comes from

One source, and it is the Commission’s own: the New Schedule of Fees made under the Companies and Allied Matters Act and published as S.I. No. 6 in the Federal Republic of Nigeria Official Gazette No. 92, Vol. 112, Lagos, 29 May 2025, signed by the Minister of Industry, Trade and Investment and dated 31 October 2024.

Two notes on reading it, because both tripped us up. The gazette is number 92 in volume 112, and summaries elsewhere cite it as “Gazette No. 112” by taking the volume for the number. And the schedule itself states no separate commencement date beyond the date it was made, so we are not repeating the claim, common online, that it took effect on a particular later day.

Older pages still quoting ₦10,000 to register a business name, or ₦30,000 to register a company with up to ₦1 million of share capital, predate this schedule.
