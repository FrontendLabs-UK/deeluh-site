Power of attorney

A power of attorney lets somebody act for you. Two things decide whether yours will work: how specifically you list the powers, because Nigerian courts read a power of attorney strictly and will not stretch it to cover something you did not say; and whether it touches land, which changes the document from a form into a transaction that needs a lawyer.

Last checked 23 November 2026. The points below come from Nigerian practitioners’ published commentary, not from a statute we have read — a power of attorney is governed partly by common law and partly by each state’s land instruments legislation, so the detail varies by state. General information, not legal advice.

Fill in the power of attorney

Courts read a power of attorney strictly, so a power you did not write down is a power your attorney does not have. Vagueness here is not generosity, it is a gap.

If it touches land, this template is not enough

A power of attorney that deals with land is not just a form. Published guidance is consistent on two requirements and openly divided on a third, and the third is the expensive one:

  • Stamping, then registration. Where a power of attorney confers an interest in land it is stamped and registered, and the stamping comes first. Whether yours is registrable, and where, depends on the Land Instruments Registration Law of the state the land is in.
  • The powers must be specific. A general power will be read strictly against the person relying on it, which is the opposite of what people assume when they choose “general”.
  • The Governor’s consent — and here the commentary genuinely disagrees. Some practitioners say a power of attorney authorising a sublease or sale of state land, or dealing with a certificate of occupancy, needs the Governor’s consent. Others argue it does not, because the power itself transfers no interest — only the conveyance that follows does. We are not going to pick a side for you on a question that can void a land transaction. Take advice before using a power of attorney to deal with land.

Who has to witness it

Signed in Nigeria: your signature should be witnessed by at least one adult who is not a party to the document. Attestation by a notary public, judge or magistrate is not strictly required, but it supports the presumption that the document was properly executed — which is worth having if anybody later disputes it.

Signed outside Nigeria: published guidance says it should be witnessed by a notary public, or by a judge or magistrate. If you are abroad, do not sign it in front of a friend and post it.

What each clause is doing

The appointment

Names you, names your attorney, and says you appoint them. Full names and addresses, because this document will be read by a bank or a registry that has never met either of you.

The powers

The heart of it. List acts, not intentions: “operate account number … at …”, not “handle my finances”. A bank will decline what the document does not plainly cover, and a court will not read in what you left out.

Ratification

Your promise to stand behind what your attorney lawfully does under the power. It is standard, and it is what makes a third party comfortable dealing with them.

Duration and revocation

Say when it starts, and whether it ends on a date or continues until revoked. Revoking it is your act, but a third party who has not been told may still rely on it — so revocation means telling the bank, the registry or the tenant, not just telling your attorney.

Execution

You sign and date, and your witness gives their name, address and occupation. An unsigned or undated power of attorney is not a power of attorney.

One tax question we have left open

Being confirmed — stamp duty on a power of attorney

The duty table in the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 that we read lists 48 instruments and does not name a power of attorney among them. We are not drawing the conclusion that none is payable: it may fall under the table’s entry for agreements, and guidance elsewhere says a power of attorney conferring an interest in land is stamped before registration. So this is a question for a lawyer rather than an answer from us. Our stamp duty page sets out the rates the Act does give.

Where this comes from

Nigerian practitioners’ published commentary on powers of attorney, read on 23 November 2026, for the witnessing requirements, the strict construction, the stamping-and-registration position for land, and the division of opinion on the Governor’s consent. The stamp duty table is from the Nigeria Tax Act 2025. Nothing here is drawn from a statute on powers of attorney, because the governing rules are partly common law and partly each state’s land instruments legislation.
